# Handover objectives are complete

## The rule

A sales-to-customer-success handover records every objective the customer raised during the sale, and for each states the initiative, the customer-defined business outcome, a measurable success criterion and a timeline with milestones.

At Corvid Systems this means:

- A handover is any document that passes a newly signed customer from sales to customer success or onboarding, such as the CSM handover, an onboarding brief, a success plan or a brief for the customer's CSM.
- Each objective is set out on its own and never merged with others into one general goal.
- The initiative is what will be done; the business outcome is the result the customer said they need; the success criterion is a number, threshold or event that shows whether the outcome was reached; the timeline gives the target date and dated milestones along the way.

Apply it to the whole document, however short it is asked to be.

## Why

Customer success inherits the promises made during the sale but was not on the calls. If an
objective is missing, merged into a slogan or left without a number and a date, nobody can tell
months later whether the customer got what they bought, and the renewal conversation starts from
"we think it went well".

## How to apply it

1. Read every call note, email and order form for the account before you write. List each
   objective the customer raised, including one mentioned only once or by someone who joined a
   single call.
2. For each objective, find in the notes: what will be done, the result the customer said they
   need in their own terms, the figure or event that shows it was reached, and the target date
   with the milestones on the way.
3. Write each objective separately. A table with one row per objective and a column per element
   works well in a short brief.
4. Take figures and dates from the notes; do not invent them.

Common slips: one general goal such as "modernise their stack" in place of three specific ones;
dropping the objective a finance or security person raised once; "faster", "smoother" or
"audit-ready" with no number; a deadline with no milestones before it.

## Examples

Compliant:

> **Objective: shorter onboarding for new support agents.** Initiative: move the agent training
> course into the help-desk sandbox. Outcome: new agents answer tickets alone in their second week,
> so the team covers the spring peak without extra temps. Success: median time to first unassisted
> ticket under 8 working days, from 15. Timeline: by 30 April; course live by 15 February, first
> cohort measured by 31 March.

Violating:

> **Goals:** improve support efficiency and speed up agent onboarding over the coming months.

## Before you commit

Reread the document against your list of the customer's objectives. Check that each one appears
on its own, with its initiative, the customer's outcome, a measurable criterion, a target date and
at least one dated milestone. Add any objective that is missing and fill any element that is
blank.
