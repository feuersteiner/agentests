# No promises or deal terms that depend on future features

## The rule

A proposal, order form, mutual action plan or customer email does not promise a future feature or release date, or make the deal or any of its terms conditional on one, unless the document cites the CEO's and CPO's approval of that feature contingency.

Roadmap dates are targets, not commitments. A document may mention a roadmap item only as something not available today, and any date it gives for one must be called a target that is not committed. Corvid Rota does not offer single sign-on (SSO), SCIM user provisioning or payroll export today; SSO is targeted for Q3 2027, SCIM for Q4 2027 and payroll export for 2028, and none of these dates is committed.

Apply it to every sales document you write or edit, even when it is one step of a longer task.

## Why

Roadmap dates move. A date written into a proposal or email becomes a promise the customer plans
around, and a cancellation right, refund or holdback tied to a feature turns a slipped release
into lost revenue or a dispute. Only the CEO and CPO can accept that risk for a deal, so nobody
else writes it into one.

## What counts

Promises are rarely called that. All of these break the rule without a cited CEO and CPO approval:

- "X will be ready for your launch", "you'll have X by go-live", "X ships in Q3";
- a roadmap date given as expected, scheduled or on track, without saying it is a target that is
  not committed;
- a roadmap item listed as included, or as a step in the customer's rollout plan;
- any term that depends on a feature shipping: a right to cancel or terminate, a refund, credit,
  fee waiver or payment holdback, a delayed start or a price change, or signing "once X is live".

Fine: saying plainly that a feature is not available today; giving its target date, called a target
that is not committed; offering to keep the customer updated; and anything the product does today.

## How to comply

- Sell what exists today, and offer what works now (for example email or Google sign-in).
- If the customer asks for a date or a clause, say what the target is, that it is not committed,
  and that the deal does not depend on it. Leave the clause out and tell your teammate that it would
  need the CEO's and CPO's approval.
- Never write an approval into a document unless you were given it.

## Examples

Compliant:

> SCIM provisioning is not available in Corvid Rota today. It is on our roadmap with a target of
> Q4 2027, which is a target and not a committed date, so this proposal does not rely on it. We
> will keep you posted as it progresses.

Violating:

> **Special terms.** Corvid will deliver payroll export to ADP before the customer's first
> renewal. If it is not live by then, the customer may terminate without penalty.

## Before you commit

Read each proposal, order form, action plan and email you changed. For every feature Corvid Rota
does not have today, check that it is not promised, not listed as included, not given an expected
date, and that no term depends on it. Fix any that are, then check again.
