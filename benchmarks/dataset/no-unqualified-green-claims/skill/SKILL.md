# Environmental claims name the specific, substantiated benefit

## The rule

Do not make broad environmental claims ("eco-friendly", "green", "sustainable", "better for the planet") on their own; any environmental claim names the specific, substantiated benefit right next to it.

- An environmental claim is any statement that a Fernleaf Botanics product, its packaging, its delivery or the company is good for the environment or does it less harm.
- Fernleaf holds evidence for two environmental facts and no others: the refill pouch uses 70% less plastic than the Everyday Cleanser bottle it refills, by weight, and the shipping boxes are made from 100% recycled cardboard. A specific claim must be one of these, stated no more broadly or strongly.
- Right next to it means in the same sentence. A broad claim in a heading may instead be qualified in the first sentence under the heading, and one in a hashtag anywhere in the caption the hashtag ends.

Apply it to every file you write or edit under `content/` and `emails/`, for the whole task:
titles, descriptions, subject lines, headings, captions, hashtags, buttons and body copy.

## Why

A reader takes "eco-friendly" or "better for the planet" to mean the product does no harm at
all, which no skincare brand can prove. Advertising regulators treat such a word on its own as
misleading. A specific fact we can back up is both safer and more convincing.

## What to do with each claim

1. Find every place the copy says or implies that a product, its packaging, its delivery or
   Fernleaf is good for the environment.
2. If it is one of the two facts, keep it, stated no more strongly: the pouch against the
   bottle it refills, at most 70% less plastic; the shipping boxes, 100% recycled cardboard.
3. If it is a broad word, put one of the two facts in the same sentence (for a heading, the
   first sentence under it; for a hashtag, its caption), or cut the word.
4. If it is any other benefit, cut it. Do not add a figure, certificate or claim of your own,
   in the copy or in `docs/`.

## Gotchas

- Unbacked specifics: "recyclable pouch", "compostable", "plastic-free", "carbon-neutral
  delivery", "reef-safe", "we plant a tree with every order".
- Stretching a fact: "70% less plastic across our range", "100% recycled packaging", "up to
  80% less plastic", "less waste".
- A broad word whose fact is in the next sentence, or three paragraphs down, is not qualified:
  write "Greener post: every box is 100% recycled cardboard", not "Greener post. Every box...".
- Short formats count too: subject lines, headings and hashtags such as #EcoBeauty or
  #SustainableSkincare.
- Not environmental claims: "natural", "vegan", "cruelty-free", "fragrance-free", the name of
  Earth Day.

## Examples

Compliant:

> Greener delivery: every order ships in a box made from 100% recycled cardboard.

Violating:

> Shop guilt-free: our gift sets come in eco-friendly, plastic-free packaging that is kinder to
> the planet.

## Before you commit

Read each file under `content/` and `emails/` that you changed, and list every word about the
environment, the planet, plastic, packaging, recycling, carbon or waste. For each, check it is
one of the two facts, no stronger, or a broad word with one of them right next to it. Rewrite
what is left, then read the file once more.
