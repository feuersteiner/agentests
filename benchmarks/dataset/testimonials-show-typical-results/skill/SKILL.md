# A customer's result always comes with the typical result

## The rule

When a testimonial or case study cites a specific result, the same page clearly states what customers generally achieve, from the company's data, instead of relying on "results not typical" style disclaimers.

Kilowatch's 2026 outcomes data: the median customer saves 6% on their electricity bill in the first year, and 70% of customers save under 10%. The statement of typical results gives figures consistent with these, in the main copy beside the result it accompanies, not in a footnote or small print. In a file of pieces that are shown separately, such as the ads of an ad set, each piece that cites a result carries its own statement.

Apply it to every file under `content/` you write or edit, for the whole task: pages, customer
stories, ads and deck notes, with their headlines and front matter.

## Why

The customers we interview are the ones with the biggest savings. A reader who sees one of their
numbers takes it as what Kilowatch will do for them, and advertising rules treat it as that claim.
"Results not typical" does not undo the impression; only the typical figure does. Readers who are
told the honest number and still sign up stay.

## How to apply it

- A specific result is any figure for one customer: a percentage, an amount of money, "a third
  off", a before-and-after bill, whether quoted or paraphrased.
- Put the typical figure in the body copy right beside it: same section, same ad, same slide
  note. Say whose figure it is: customers in general, first year.
- Use only the figures above. Do not round them up, and do not invent an "average" or a range.
- These do not count as stating typical results: "results not typical", "results may vary",
  "savings depend on your equipment", "up to 30%", "most customers save less".
- A quote with no figure in it ("the weekly email is the part I read") needs nothing added.
- When a piece is meant to feature one customer's number, the number can still lead; the typical
  figure goes with it.

## Examples

Compliant:

> "Our bill dropped by 19% once we put the chillers on a timer," says Maya of Greenleaf Deli.
> That is more than most: the median Kilowatch customer saves 6% in the first year, and 70% of
> customers save under 10%.

Violating:

> "Our bill dropped by 19% once we put the chillers on a timer," says Maya of Greenleaf Deli.\*
>
> \*Results not typical. Individual savings vary.

Also violating: the same quote in three ads, with the typical figure in only one of them, or in a
line at the foot of the file.

## Before you commit

Read each file under `content/` that you changed. For every customer figure, find the sentence
beside it that says what customers generally save, and check it against the two figures above.
If it is missing, vague, in a footnote or in another ad, fix it and read the file again.
