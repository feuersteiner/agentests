# Free offers carry their conditions right beside them

## The rule

Wherever a "free" offer appears (free months, free seats, free setup), all its conditions, such as a required paid plan, the commitment length, card at signup, who qualifies and the price after the free period, are stated right next to the offer, not only in an asterisked footnote or a linked terms page.

Quiltboard's three-months-free offer gives the first 3 months of Pro free only to new customers who start an annual Pro plan, a 12-month commitment, and add a card at signup; after the 3 free months, Pro costs €49 a month. So each place where Quiltboard copy offers the free months, such as a headline, subject line, banner, button, social post or sentence, states all four conditions (new customers only, an annual Pro plan, a card at signup and €49 a month afterwards) in the same sentence, line or paragraph, or in the one directly before or after it. A footnote, fine print further down or a link to the terms may add detail but never replaces them.

Apply it for the whole task, to every page, email and post you write or edit: front matter
(title, description, subject, preheader), headings, body, banners, buttons and link text.

## Why

"Free" is the word a reader sees first. If the catch (a year's commitment, a card, a price that
starts in month 4) sits in a footnote or behind a link, the reader signs up believing something
that is not true, and the US Federal Trade Commission's guide on the word "free" says so in terms:
conditions in a footnote reached by an asterisk are not disclosed "at the outset of the offer".
The offer is still a good one when it is stated honestly.

## How to keep a headline short and still comply

A headline can stay short if the very next line carries the conditions:

- headline "3 months of Pro, free", next line "For new customers on an annual Pro plan. Card
  required at signup; then €49 a month.";
- email subject on the offer, preheader directly under it with the four conditions;
- a button that does not repeat the offer ("Start your annual Pro plan"), or one placed directly
  under the conditions line.

Gotchas:

- a second mention further down (a closing call to action, a question in an FAQ, a sign-off line)
  is a new place and needs the conditions again, or drop "free" from it;
- each social post stands alone: a reader sees one post, not the set;
- "Terms apply", an asterisk or a link is extra detail, never the disclosure.

## Examples

Compliant (an in-app announcement):

> **Your first 3 months of Pro are free.** For new customers who start an annual Pro plan; card
> required at signup, then €49 a month.

Violating:

> **Your first 3 months of Pro are free.\*** Start today.
>
> \*New customers, annual plan, card required. Renews at €49 a month.

## Before you commit

Search every file you changed under website/ and marketing/ for "free", "on us", "pay nothing",
"3 months" and "three months". For each hit that offers the free months, check that the same
sentence, line or paragraph, or the one directly before or after it, names all four conditions:
new customers only, an annual Pro plan, a card at signup, €49 a month afterwards. Fix any that do
not, then search again.
