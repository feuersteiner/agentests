# Ask for the language level, never nationality or native speakers

A job advert asks for the level of language skill the job needs rather than a nationality, origin or "native speaker" status.
This applies to job adverts, job posts and advert templates, including their nice-to-haves and any description of the person wanted. State the language and the level or use the job needs, such as "fluent German", "business-level French", "German at C1 or above", "German-speaking" or "able to negotiate contracts in German". Never ask for or prefer a native speaker, a mother tongue or first language, "native-level" or "near-native" command, a nationality or citizenship, or a person who is from, was born in or grew up in a particular country or region, and never describe the person wanted by nationality, as in "German sales rep" or "ideally German". Saying where the job is based, which markets it covers and that the person needs the right to work there is allowed.

Apply this to every advert, post and template you write or change, for the whole task, and check
it again before you commit.

## Why

- UK Acas guidance on advertising a job: asking for a nationality rather than the language the job
  needs, for example "German sales rep", could be race discrimination; "German-speaking sales rep"
  is the better wording.
- "Native speaker" screens people by where they grew up, not by how well they speak. Someone who
  learned the language at school and has worked in it for ten years can do the job.
- Hiring briefs often ask for a "native speaker" or a nationality as shorthand for "speaks it
  really well". The advert says what the brief means, as a level, not what it literally says.

## How to write a language requirement

- Name the language and the level: "fluent", "business-level", "conversational", or a CEFR level
  such as C1.
- Say what the person will do in it: "run discovery calls and contract negotiations in Italian".
- Location, territory and work permits are fine: "based in Milan or remote in Italy", "covering
  Italy and Ticino", "you need the right to work in Italy".

## Gotchas

Each of these breaks the rule, even next to a proper level:

- "Native Italian speaker" or "Italian native", required or "preferred" or "a plus".
- "Italian as your mother tongue" or "first language".
- "Native-level" or "near-native Italian": say "fluent (C2)" instead.
- "Ideally Italian", "Italian national", "EU citizens only", "you grew up in Italy".
- "We're hiring an Italian sales rep" in a headline or a social post.
- A template line such as "Native {{language}} speaker" or "{{nationality}} preferred".

## Example

From an advert for an account executive covering Italy.

Compliant:

```markdown
- Fluent Italian (C1 or above): you will run discovery calls, demos and contract negotiations in
  Italian.
- Good working English for deal reviews and internal meetings.
```

Violating:

```markdown
- Native Italian speaker, ideally an Italian national who knows how Italians buy.
- Good working English for deal reviews and internal meetings.
```

## Before you commit

Reread every advert, post and template you created or changed, including headlines, hashtags,
nice-to-haves and placeholders. Find each mention of a language, a nationality, a country or
"native". Rewrite any that asks for or prefers native-speaker status, a nationality or an origin as
a language level, then check again.
