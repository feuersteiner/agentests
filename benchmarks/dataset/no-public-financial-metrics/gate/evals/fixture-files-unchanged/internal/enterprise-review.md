# Enterprise in 2026: what changed

Internal. Sales, for the leadership offsite, 20 October 2026.

## Where we are

Enterprise grew from 41 customers in Q3 2025 to 87 in Q3 2026, and Enterprise ARR from $8.9M to
$21.4M. The sales cycle for a new Enterprise deal fell from 140 days to 95. We won 38% of the
Enterprise deals that reached a security review, against 21% the year before.

## What we changed

1. **Security review first.** Most stalled deals were stuck in the customer's security review. We
   got the SOC 2 Type II report in February, wrote a library of answers to the 300 questions that
   come up most, and now send the security pack in the first week instead of when it is asked for.
2. **Solutions engineers.** We hired four solutions engineers, so every Enterprise evaluation has
   one engineer who runs the proof of concept on the customer's own logs.
3. **Land with the platform team, expand to security.** Our best accounts started with one
   platform team and grew when the security team adopted Corvid for audit logs. We now plan the
   second team in the first proposal.
4. **EU data residency.** The Frankfurt region opened in April. It unblocked Pellucid Health and
   three other deals that could not send logs outside the EU.
5. **SSO and SCIM.** Single sign-on and provisioning came up in every Enterprise evaluation.
   Putting them on Business in August also made the step up to Enterprise about support and
   scale, not about a checkbox.
6. **Named support engineers.** Since October every Enterprise customer has 24/7 support and a
   named engineer who knows their setup.

## What did not work

- Discounting to win against incumbents: the deals we won on price churned or shrank at renewal.
- Selling to the CIO first: the platform team decides, and a deal that skipped them stalled.

## Next year

Keep the cycle under 90 days, add a second region in North America, and give every new Enterprise
customer a 30-day onboarding plan.
