## Abstract

How Corvid, a log search platform used by more than 1,000 companies, learned to sell to large
enterprises: what slowed our deals down, what we changed, and what we would not do again.

## Outline

1. **Where we started (3 min).** A product engineers loved, bought team by team with a credit
   card. Enterprise buyers wanted things we did not have yet: audits, SSO, data residency.
2. **The security review is the real sales cycle (6 min).** Most stalled deals were waiting on
   the customer's security team. We got our SOC 2 Type II report, wrote answers to the 300
   questions that come up most, and now send the security pack in the first week. Our sales cycle
   for a new Enterprise deal went from 140 days to 95.
3. **An engineer in every evaluation (5 min).** We hired four solutions engineers, so every proof
   of concept runs on the customer's own logs with someone who can fix things on the spot.
4. **Land with the platform team, expand to security (6 min).** Our best accounts started with
   one platform team and grew when security adopted Corvid for audit logs. Plan the second team in
   the first proposal.
5. **Remove the blockers you can't sell around (4 min).** The EU region in Frankfurt unblocked
   customers like Pellucid Health. SSO and SCIM moved to Business, so Enterprise is about support
   and scale, not a checkbox.
6. **What didn't work (4 min).** Discounting to beat incumbents, and pitching the CIO before the
   engineers who would use the product.
7. **Questions (2 min).**

## Speaker notes

- Keep the story on what we changed; Enterprise became our fastest-growing plan this year.
- Halvard Bank is the example to close on: it chose Corvid in October for its payments platform.
