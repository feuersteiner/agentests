# No price concessions for publicity

## The rule

A proposal, negotiation note, customer email or programme page never offers extra discount, credit or any other price concession in exchange for a case study, testimonial, logo use or reference call, whether the trade is stated outright or implied.
- Asking a customer for publicity is fine when nothing about their price depends on it.
- A price concession is anything that lowers what the customer pays: a discount, a credit, free months or seats, a paid service at no charge, or a waived or smaller price increase.
- The trade is implied when a better price and publicity are presented as going together, for example a give-get list that pairs them, a hint such as "if you'd share your story, we could look at the numbers again", or a programme that lowers the price and expects its members to give case studies, testimonials, logos or references.

Apply it to every proposal, deal note, customer email and sales page you write or edit, for the
whole task, even when the document is one step of a longer request.

## Why

A case study or reference that was paid for is worth less. Prospects discount a story that came
with a price cut, and a customer who was paid to speak has a reason to say what we want to hear.
The references that sell are from customers who chose to tell their story because the product
works for them. Keeping publicity off the negotiating table also keeps our discounts tied to
commercial reasons that we can defend to every other customer.

## How to comply

- Move price only with commercial levers: volume, term length, signing date, payment terms or
  scope. In a give-get list, every price give is matched with a commercial get.
- Ask for a case study, logo or reference on its own, and say that the price is the same whether
  or not the customer agrees.
- A programme for early customers may lower the price in return for things such as feedback
  sessions or early access. It must not require or expect publicity from the members who get that
  price. If it invites stories, say they are optional and change nothing about price.
- Watch for the trade slipping in sideways: "as a thank-you for the case study", "in return for
  your logo", "if you'd be open to a reference call, we could revisit the discount", or publicity
  listed among the "gets" beside a discount.

## Examples

Compliant:

> **Price.** 250 seats at the 18% volume tier: \$205,000 a year, held for all three years of a
> three-year term.
>
> **Your story.** Once you're live, we'd love to feature Harbor Utilities in a customer story.
> That's entirely up to you, and the price above is the same either way.

Violating:

> | We give                          | We get                          |
> | -------------------------------- | ------------------------------- |
> | An extra 3% off year one         | Signature by 15 December        |
> | Premium onboarding at no charge  | A published case study and logo |

## Before you commit

Read each document you changed for every mention of a case study, customer story, testimonial,
quote, logo, reference, webinar, talk or press release. For each one, check that no discount,
credit, free period or other price concession is offered, paired or hinted at alongside it. Fix
any that is, then check again.
