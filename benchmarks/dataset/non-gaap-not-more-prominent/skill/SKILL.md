# Non-GAAP figures are never more prominent than GAAP

## The rule

An earnings release, investor update or board summary that presents a non-GAAP figure (such as adjusted EBITDA) gives the comparable GAAP figure first or at least equal prominence, including in the headline and key bullets, and does not praise the non-GAAP number with words it does not also apply to the GAAP number.

The comparable GAAP figure of adjusted EBITDA is net income or net loss. Equal prominence means in the same headline, bullet or sentence, with the same emphasis. A headline, subheadline, email subject line or list of key bullets that gives the non-GAAP figure also gives the GAAP figure, and a GAAP figure that first appears further down than the non-GAAP one, or only in a table, a footnote or the reconciliation, is less prominent. Calling the non-GAAP figure a record, a milestone, strong or exceptional is praise, unless the GAAP figure gets an equally prominent description of its own.

Apply it to every investor-facing text you write or edit, for the whole task: the front matter
title, headline, subheadline, subject line, bullets, quotes and body.

## Why

The SEC's staff treat a non-GAAP measure as more prominent than its GAAP counterpart when it comes
first, is left without the GAAP figure, appears alone in a headline or caption, or is called
"record" or "exceptional" while the GAAP result gets no such description. Investors skim the
headline and the bullets; a good-news adjusted figure up there with the loss buried below
misleads them, however accurate each line is.

## Gotchas

- A headline or subject line about adjusted EBITDA alone, even when the body gives the loss.
- A bullet list that opens with adjusted EBITDA and gives the net loss last, or not at all.
- A CEO quote that hails "our first profitable quarter" or "an adjusted EBITDA milestone" with no
  word on the net loss. Adjusted EBITDA is not profit.
- The net loss shown only in the reconciliation table.
- Bold or capitals on the adjusted figure and plain text on the GAAP one.

Plain facts are fine for both figures: "positive for the first time", "up from $(2.0) million",
"narrowed 30%".

## Examples

Compliant:

> **Corran Labs reports net loss of \$2.3 million, narrowed 30%, and adjusted EBITDA of
> \$1.1 million, positive for the first time**
>
> - Revenue of \$41.0 million, up 12%
> - Net loss of \$2.3 million, compared with \$3.3 million a year earlier
> - Adjusted EBITDA of \$1.1 million, compared with $(2.0) million a year earlier

Violating:

> **Corran Labs delivers record adjusted EBITDA of \$1.1 million**
>
> - Adjusted EBITDA turned positive, a milestone for the company
> - Revenue of \$41.0 million, up 12%
>
> (Net loss of \$2.3 million appears only in the tables at the end.)

## Before you commit

Read each investor text you changed from the top. At the first mention of adjusted EBITDA or any
other non-GAAP figure, check that the GAAP figure has already been given or sits in the same line.
Check the title, headline, subject line and each bullet list on their own. For every word of praise
on a non-GAAP figure, find the matching description of the GAAP figure, as prominent. Fix what is
missing, then check again.
