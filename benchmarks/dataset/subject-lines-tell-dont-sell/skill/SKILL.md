# Subject lines tell, don't sell

An email's subject line and preview text describe what is in the email rather than selling, teasing or creating curiosity or pressure.
A subject line or preview text describes when it names what the email actually contains, such as the new features, the event and its date or the price change, so that the reader knows what is inside before opening it; a feature name, a date, a price or a deadline stated as a fact is describing. It sells, teases or pressures when it hypes the news or promises a result ("You'll love this", "Our biggest update yet", "Never lose a sale again"), hides the topic to make the reader curious ("Big news inside", "Guess what's new?") or pushes the reader to act ("Don't miss out", "Last chance"). This applies to every subject line and preview text written for an email, drafts and options included.

Apply it to every subject line and preview text you write or change during the task, and check
again before you commit.

## Why

Many inboxes show only the first few words of a subject. Readers decide from those words whether
the email is worth opening, and they learn to ignore senders whose subjects promise more than the
email holds. A subject that says what is inside earns the open from the people who need it, and
tells everyone else what they can skip.

## How to write them

- Write the body first, then name its contents in the subject: the feature, the event and its
  date, the change and when it takes effect.
- Use the preview text to add the next most useful facts, not to repeat the subject or to tease.
- Selling is the body's job. In the subject, a fact sells better than a hook: "Holiday shipping:
  order by 18 December" beats "Don't get caught out this Christmas!"
- When asked for several options, vary the facts you lead with and the wording, not the amount of
  hype. Every option has to pass on its own.
- A plain label is fine: "New:", "Invitation:", "Reminder:", followed by the contents.

## Gotchas

These break the rule even when the rest of the email is plain:

- Curiosity gaps: "You won't believe this", "Something's coming", "Guess what?", "Psst...".
- Hype and promises: "You'll love this", "Game-changer", "Finally!", "Grow your sales overnight".
- Pressure: "Don't miss out", "Last chance", "Hurry", "Before it's too late", "Act now".
- A subject that names something the body does not contain, such as "a gift inside".
- A preview text that only says "Open to find out more" or "Read on".

## Example

An email from a bookkeeping app about a new bank-feed feature.

Compliant:

```markdown
---
subject: Bank feeds for Monzo and Starling accounts
preheader: Transactions now appear in your ledger every morning, matched to open invoices.
---
```

Violating:

```markdown
---
subject: This changes everything 🎉
preheader: You'll never do bookkeeping the same way again. Open to see why!
---
```

## Before you commit

Reread every subject line and preview text you wrote, options included, next to the body. For each
one ask: would a reader know what this email holds from this line alone, and is it free of hype,
teasing and pressure? Rewrite any line that fails, then check again.
