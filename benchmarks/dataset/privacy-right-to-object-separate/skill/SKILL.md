# The right to object gets its own heading

## The rule

Where the company relies on legitimate interests or sends direct marketing, the privacy notice tells readers about their right to object explicitly, clearly and separately from other rights and information (its own heading or box, with how to use it), not as one item in a list of rights.

Tessellate relies on legitimate interests for product analytics and fraud prevention, and sends marketing emails, so this holds for every Tessellate privacy notice, including a short notice for a signup form and a privacy FAQ, where the right to object gets its own question. The right to object may also be listed among the other rights, as long as it has its own heading or box as well.

Apply it to every privacy text you write or change, for the whole task, including when you shorten
or reorganise one.

## Why

UK GDPR says the right to object must be brought to people's attention explicitly, clearly and
separately from any other information (ICO guidance on the right to be informed). It is the one
right that lets people stop processing they never agreed to, such as analytics, fraud checks and
marketing, so it may not be buried among the others.

## What counts as separate

- A heading such as "Your right to object", or a sub-heading for it inside "Your rights".
- A box or callout, or a paragraph of its own that starts with a bold label naming it.
- In a FAQ, its own question, such as "Can I stop you using my data for analytics or marketing?"

It says what readers can object to (analytics and fraud prevention, and marketing emails or the
newsletter) and how: email privacy@tessellate.example, the unsubscribe link in every marketing
email, or the "Product news" switch under Settings > Notifications.

Not separate: one bullet in a list of rights, a clause in "you can access, correct, delete or
object", a sentence inside the table of purposes or a "Your choices" paragraph, or a bold word in
the rights list.

## Examples

Compliant:

> ## Your right to object
>
> You can object at any time to our use of your data for product analytics or fraud prevention:
> email privacy@tessellate.example. You can also stop marketing emails at any time with the
> unsubscribe link at the bottom of each one.

Violating:

> ## Your rights
>
> You can ask to access, correct, delete or move your data, restrict how we use it, or object to
> our use of it. Email privacy@tessellate.example.

## Before you commit

Open each privacy text you changed. Find where it tells readers they can object. If that is only a
bullet or a clause among other rights, or it is missing, give it its own heading, box or FAQ
question that says what they can object to and how, then check again.
