# Referral fees: due only after the customer pays and the partner invoices

## The rule

A Corvid Systems message, FAQ, overview or agreement for referral partners never says or implies that a referral fee is owed, due or paid once a referral is accepted, once the customer signs, on a verbal win or when the deal closes; every statement of when the fee becomes due or is paid ties it to the customer's payment and the partner's invoice.

- Corvid's referral fee is 10% of the referred customer's first-year ARR (annual recurring revenue).
- The fee is due only once the deal is closed-won, Corvid has received the customer's first payment and the partner has invoiced Corvid for the fee. Corvid pays it 30 days after it receives the customer's first payment.

Apply it to every partner-facing document you write or edit, even when it is one step of a
longer task.

## Why

Partners plan their own cash flow around what we tell them. A signed order form is not money:
customers delay their first payment, renegotiate or never pay, and the fee is a share of what we
actually collect. An email that says "the fee is due now they've signed" is quoted back to us when
the money is late, and a friendly message naturally puts the reward at its most attractive moment.

## Watch for

- "Now that they've signed, your fee is due" or "your fee is locked in".
- "Paid on close", "paid when the deal is won", "paid with this month's payout".
- A date ("on 15 December", "by the end of the month") with no condition on the customer's
  payment.
- A timing that names the customer's payment but forgets the partner's invoice.
- Saying the customer has paid, or the partner has invoiced, when the notes do not show it.

## What you can say instead

- The fee's amount, and that it becomes due once the customer has paid its first invoice to
  Corvid and the partner has invoiced Corvid, with payment 30 days after the customer's payment.
- An expected date only as a condition: "if the customer pays on its due date and you have
  invoiced us, around <that date plus 30 days>".
- That signature or closed-won alone does not make the fee due, when a partner asks.

## Examples

Compliant, a reply about a referred deal that has signed but not paid:

> Thanks for introducing Halden Hotels. Your fee is 10% of their first-year ARR, \$5,400. It
> becomes due once Halden has paid our first invoice, which is due on 9 January, and you have
> invoiced us; we pay 30 days after their payment arrives, so around 8 February if they pay on
> time.

Violating, the same reply:

> Great news: Halden signed this week, so your \$5,400 fee is confirmed. Send us your invoice and
> we'll pay it with this month's partner payout.

## Before you commit

Read each changed partner email and each changed document directly under `partners/` for every
statement of when the fee is earned, due, confirmed or paid. Check that each one waits for the
customer's payment and the partner's invoice, and that nothing claims a payment or an invoice the
notes do not show. Fix any that does not, then check again.
