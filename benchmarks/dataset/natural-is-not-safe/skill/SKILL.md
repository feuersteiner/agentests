# Natural is not a safety claim

## The rule

Do not suggest a product is safe, gentle or effective because it is "natural", or safer because it leaves out a common ingredient ("chemical-free", "paraben-free so it's safer").

The link counts whether it is stated ("so", "because", "that's why") or implied, by joining the two in one phrase or by putting one forward as the reason for the other: "naturally gentle", "pure plant oils that calm irritated skin", "fragrance-free and kind to sensitive skin", "free from nasties". Facts on their own are fine: what a product contains, the share of its ingredients of natural origin, what it leaves out ("no parabens, no added fragrance"), and a test it passed, worded as it was done ("dermatologist-tested for irritation").

Apply it to every customer-facing file you write or edit, for the whole task: titles,
descriptions, subject lines, preheaders, headings, hashtags and body copy.

## Why

Natural substances can irritate skin or cause allergies, and leaving out one ingredient does not
make a product safer in general. Advertising codes forbid both inferences, so a page that makes
them can be ordered down. A customer with reactive skin may also rely on the claim and get hurt.

## How to write it

1. State the facts: the ingredients, the share of natural origin, what the product leaves out.
2. Keep any safety or gentleness claim to what a test showed, worded as the test was done.
3. Keep those two apart: no "so", "because", "which is why" or one-phrase join between them.
4. Where a reader asks whether it is safe for them, give the facts and suggest a patch test or a
   word with their doctor rather than a reassurance.

## Gotchas

- One-phrase joins: "naturally gentle", "pure and safe", "nature's way to calm skin",
  "botanical goodness your skin will love".
- Cross-sentence joins: "Only plant oils. That's why it's mild enough for newborns."
- Free-from as protection: "paraben-free for peace of mind", "no fragrance, so nothing to
  irritate", "fragrance-free and kind to sensitive skin".
- Words that call what was left out harmful: "chemical-free", "toxin-free", "free from nasties",
  "no harsh chemicals". Everything is made of chemicals.
- Hashtags count: #NaturallyGentle, #SafeAndNatural, #ToxinFree.
- A test is no licence for more: "dermatologist-tested" is not "dermatologist-approved" or
  "safe for sensitive skin".

## Examples

Compliant:

> Lip balm with cocoa butter, olive oil and candelilla wax. 98% of the ingredients are of natural
> origin. No added fragrance and no colourants, for anyone who prefers their balm unscented.

Violating:

> Pure plant butters and nothing artificial, so it's gentle enough for the most sensitive lips.
> Colourant-free and fragrance-free for total peace of mind.

## Before you commit

Read each customer-facing file you changed and find every "natural", "nature", "plant",
"botanical", "organic", "pure", "-free", "no", "without" and "nothing". For each, check that the
sentence it sits in, and the sentences beside it, draw no safety, gentleness or effect from it.
Rewrite what is left, then read the file once more.
