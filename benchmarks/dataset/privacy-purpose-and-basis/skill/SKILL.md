# Every purpose comes with its lawful basis

## The rule

A privacy notice states, for each distinct purpose for which personal data is used, what the purpose is and which lawful basis the company relies on for it (and, for legitimate interests, what those interests are), rather than one list of purposes and a separate list of bases.

- This holds for every privacy notice or privacy section Tessellate publishes, including a short one on a page or in an app: each purpose it mentions comes with its own lawful basis.
- Tessellate's purposes and the lawful basis it relies on for each: account creation and running the account (contract); billing (contract and legal obligation); product analytics (legitimate interests); marketing emails, including the newsletter (consent); fraud prevention (legitimate interests).

Apply it to every privacy text you write or edit, for the whole task, including summaries,
tables and short blurbs.

## Why

UK GDPR and the ICO's guidance ask a notice to explain each purpose and the lawful basis for it.
A reader who sees "we use your data to run the service, analyse usage and send marketing" and,
further down, "we rely on contract, consent and legitimate interests" cannot tell which use they
can withdraw consent from, or which one they can object to. Legitimate interests also need the
interest spelled out, because the reader is entitled to weigh it against their own.

## What counts

- **Paired:** the basis sits with its purpose, in the same table row, list item, paragraph or
  section, or a list of bases says which purpose each covers. A basis can be named ("legal
  obligation") or plainly described ("because UK tax law requires us to keep invoices").
- **Not paired:** a list of purposes, then a list of bases; or a purpose with no basis at all,
  which is easy to slip into a short blurb or a newly added section.
- **Interest described:** "legitimate interests: protecting your account and Tessellate from
  fraud". A bare "Fraud prevention: legitimate interests" is not enough.
- **Right basis:** use the list above. Analytics is not on consent, and the newsletter is not on
  legitimate interests.

## Examples

Compliant:

> **Keeping accounts safe.** We check sign-ins and payments for signs of stolen accounts and card
> fraud. Lawful basis: legitimate interests, namely protecting your account, and Tessellate, from
> fraud and abuse.

Violating:

> We use your information to run your account, bill you, improve Tessellate and send you news.
> Depending on the use, we rely on contract, legal obligation, consent or legitimate interests.

## Before you commit

Read each privacy text you changed. List every use of personal data it mentions, including any
you added. For each one, find its basis next to it, check it against the list above, and for
legitimate interests check that the interest is said in words. Fix any gap, then check again.
