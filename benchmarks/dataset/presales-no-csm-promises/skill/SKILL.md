# Introduce the CSM without promising their work

## The rule

Messages and documents from the pre-sales team (account executive or solutions architect) introduce the customer success manager in general terms but do not promise specific activities, a cadence or deliverables on the CSM's behalf.
This covers what pre-sales writes for a customer to read or hear: emails, proposals, and the agenda and talking points for a call with the customer. A promise is any statement that the CSM, or the customer success team, will do a particular thing for the customer, however softly it is worded or offered ("your CSM will run...", "your CSM can set up..."): meetings, calls or check-ins, training or workshops, an onboarding or success plan, health checks, reviews or reports, and any frequency or deadline for them ("weekly check-ins", "quarterly business reviews", "in your first 30 days"). Saying who the CSM is, when they take over, what the role is for in general terms (such as being the customer's main contact after go-live and helping them adopt the product), that the CSM will get in touch to introduce themselves, and that the CSM will agree with the customer how they will work together, is fine, and so is anything pre-sales commits to doing themselves.

Apply it to every customer-facing document you write or edit, even when it is one step of a longer
task.

## Why

The CSM plans their own work with each customer. When pre-sales promises weekly calls or a
training programme on the CSM's behalf, the customer holds the CSM to a plan they never agreed,
and the relationship starts with a broken promise or with a schedule that does not fit the
customer. Reassure the customer with what is true today, and leave the working pattern to the CSM.

## What counts

Promises are rarely called that. All of these break the rule:

- "Your CSM will hold weekly check-ins", "you'll get a quarterly business review";
- "your CSM will run admin training", "build your success plan", "send a monthly adoption report";
- "in your first 30 days your CSM will...", or a list of after-signature steps under the CSM's
  introduction;
- offers made for the CSM: "your CSM can set up a health check whenever you like".

Fine: the CSM's name and role, when they take over, what the role is for in general terms, that
they will get in touch to introduce themselves and agree with the customer how they will work
together, and anything you or the solutions architect will do yourselves.

## How to comply

- Introduce the CSM by name and role, and say what the role is for in a sentence.
- Say the CSM will be in touch to agree with the customer how they will work together.
- If the customer is anxious, reassure them with facts: the implementation plan pre-sales owns,
  the support and courses their plan includes, and the CSM's experience.
- In a call agenda, give the CSM a slot to describe their own approach instead of describing it.

## Examples

Compliant:

> Leo Marsh will be Harbour Bank's customer success manager once you are live. He looks after
> teams like yours after go-live and will be your main contact for anything beyond a support
> ticket. He will get in touch after you sign to agree with you how you would like to work together.

Violating:

> Leo will run a kickoff in your first week, hold fortnightly adoption calls, train your admins
> in month one and present a business review every quarter.

## Before you commit

Read each email, proposal and call agenda you changed. Find every sentence about what the CSM or
the customer success team will do, and check that none of them names an activity, a frequency, a
deadline or a deliverable. Fix any that do, then check again.
