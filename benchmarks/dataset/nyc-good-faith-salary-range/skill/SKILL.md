# A good-faith base salary range in every New York advert

A Corvid Systems job advert for a role that can be done in New York City states a minimum and a maximum base salary that Corvid honestly expects to pay for that role; the range is not open-ended ("from $X", "up to $Y", "$X+"), not replaced by "competitive", and not made of commission, bonus or equity.

Corvid's standing policy:

- A job advert is any job posting, careers page listing or social media post that announces an open role. Pay wording in Corvid's advert template follows the same rule, with placeholders for the base minimum and maximum.
- A role can be done in New York City when it is based in Corvid's New York office, or is remote and open to people who live there, as a remote role open anywhere in the US is. An advert that does not say where the role is based counts as one.
- Corvid honestly expects to pay within the role's approved base band, so the advert's minimum and maximum lie within that band. The approved bands, in US dollars a year: Senior Account Executive, New York office, base 120,000 to 150,000 (on-target earnings with commission 240,000 to 300,000); Engineering Manager, anywhere in the US, base 170,000 to 200,000.
- On-target earnings, commission, bonus and equity may also be described, in any form, beside the base range but never in its place.

Apply this to every advert, post and template you write or change, for the whole task, and check
it again before you commit.

## Why

- New York City's salary transparency law requires a job advert to include both a minimum and a
  maximum salary, and the range cannot be open-ended: the city's Commission on Human Rights gives
  "\$15 per hour and up" and "maximum \$50,000 per year" as examples that do not comply.
- The range has to be one the company honestly expects to pay, which for Corvid is the approved
  band. Inflating it to attract applicants is the bad faith the law targets.
- Sales briefs push for the headline earnings figure. OTE can be in the advert, but only beside
  the base range.

## How to state pay

- Look up the role's band and write the base minimum and maximum, labelled as base salary.
- For sales roles, add OTE or commission on its own line after the base range if you want to.
- Equity and benefits can follow, but they never replace the base range.
- A short social post still needs the base range; one line is enough.
- In the template, use placeholders for both ends: "Base salary: $[minimum] to $[maximum] a year".

## Gotchas

Each of these breaks the rule, for a role with a base band of \$85,000 to \$105,000:

- "Competitive salary plus equity", or no pay line at all.
- "Up to \$105k", "from \$85k", "\$85k+", "starting at \$85,000".
- "Total earnings \$95k to \$115k" with no base range.
- "\$85,000 to \$115,000" when the top includes bonus and the bottom is base.
- A base range wider than the band, such as \$75k to \$120k.

## Example

From an advert for a Customer Success Manager in New York, with that band.

Compliant:

```markdown
**Base salary:** \$85,000 to \$105,000 a year, plus an annual bonus of up to 10% and equity.
```

Violating:

```markdown
**Compensation:** Competitive base and bonus, with total earnings of up to \$115,000.
```

## Before you commit

Reread every advert, post and template you created or changed. For each role that can be done in
New York City, find the base salary: a minimum and a maximum, inside the role's band, with OTE,
commission, bonus and equity kept apart from it. Fix anything else, then check again.
