# Lullo helps people wind down; it does not treat anything

## The rule

Copy for a wellness product that is not a licensed medicine or certified medical device must not claim or imply that the product diagnoses, treats or prevents a disease or condition, physical or mental, such as insomnia, anxiety or depression.

- Lullo is a wellness app, not a licensed medicine or a certified medical device.
- A condition includes its symptoms and its everyday names, such as sleepless nights, trouble sleeping, panic attacks or low mood. Curing, fixing, ending, beating, relieving or reducing a condition all count as treating it, even when softened with "can help" or "may", and so does presenting Lullo as the answer to a condition.
- Saying what Lullo does, and that it helps people relax, wind down or fall asleep more easily, is fine. Everyday stress or a busy mind at bedtime is not a condition. Naming a condition only to say that Lullo does not treat it, or to suggest seeing a doctor, is fine.

Apply it to every page, ad and email you write or edit, for the whole task: body copy,
headlines, ad lines, buttons and the front matter (title, description, subject, preheader).

## Why

Advertising rules let only licensed medicines and certified medical devices claim to treat or
prevent a condition. A sleep or breathing app that promises to fix insomnia or anxiety breaks
them, and it can keep someone who needs care from seeking it.

## What counts

These are claims, hedged or not:

- "beat insomnia", "ease anxiety", "relief from panic attacks", "masks tinnitus";
- "no more sleepless nights", "stop lying awake", "fix your sleep problems";
- a sleep problem or condition as the hook, then Lullo as the fix: "Can't sleep? Get Lullo",
  "Anxious at night? Try our breathing course";
- "keeps low mood at bay", "find out whether you have insomnia";
- the same with "may", "can help", "designed to", or a survey figure dressed up as one.

These are fine: what the sounds, exercises, timer and course do; relaxing, unwinding, winding down
and falling asleep more easily; "64% of listeners said they fell asleep more easily", as the
survey asked it; a long day or a busy mind at bedtime; sleep-habit tips that are not offered as a
cure; and pointing someone with an ongoing sleep or mental-health problem to a doctor.

Search terms and survey answers often name a problem ("trouble getting to sleep"). Write to the
moment instead: the evening, the wind-down, the sounds and the breathing.

## Examples

Compliant:

> **Put the day down.** Ten minutes of slow breathing and the sound of rain on a tent, then the
> timer fades it out while you drift off.

Violating:

> **Lying awake again?** Lullo's breathing exercises calm anxiety and help you beat insomnia for
> good.

## Before you commit

Read each page, ad and email you changed, front matter and headlines included. Mark every
condition or symptom the copy names. For each, check that the copy does not say or suggest Lullo
fixes, eases, prevents or detects it. Rewrite each such line around what Lullo does, then check
again.
