# No age-coded or other protected-group preference in job adverts

A job advert does not show a preference for, or use wording that would discourage, applicants because of age or another protected characteristic, for example "recent graduates", "digital native", "young and energetic team", or a cap on years of experience the job does not need.
- It covers job adverts, posts announcing a job, and the sections reused in adverts, including how they describe the team.
- The other protected characteristics are sex, gender identity, sexual orientation, pregnancy, race, colour, national origin, religion, disability and genetic information.
- An upper limit on years of experience, such as "no more than 5 years" or "2 to 4 years", is such a cap: ask for a minimum or for skills instead.
- A job level in a title, such as "Junior" or "Senior", describes the job and is allowed.

Apply this to every advert, post and shared section you write or change, for the whole task, and
check it again before you commit.

## Why

- The US EEOC says an advert that seeks "recent college graduates" may discourage people over 40
  and may break the law; the same holds for sex, race, religion, national origin, disability and
  the other protected characteristics. EU and UK equality law say the same.
- Hiring briefs and culture notes often describe the person the manager pictures ("young and
  hungry", "one of the lads", "2-4 years max"). An advert keeps the job's needs and drops that
  picture.
- Describing the team by who its people are ("we're all in our twenties") tells everyone else they
  would not fit. Describe what the team does and how it works instead.

## How to write it instead

- Turn each wish about the person into the skill or result behind it: "gets TikTok" becomes "you
  have run paid campaigns on TikTok and can show the results".
- State experience as a minimum or as skills and a portfolio, never as a maximum or a range.
- Describe the team by its work, its pace and its habits: how it ships, reviews and makes decisions.
- Address the reader as "you"; refer to the candidate as "they", never "he" or "she".
- Ask for a language at a level of fluency, not for a native speaker or a nationality.

## Gotchas

Each of these breaks the rule, though none says "we only hire young people":

- "Perfect for recent graduates", "fresh out of uni", "early-twenties energy".
- "Digital native", "Gen Z", "you grew up on social media".
- "You'll fit right in with our young, high-energy crew."
- "3-5 years of experience" or "no more than 6 years in the industry".
- "Most of us joined straight from university" in the About the team section.
- "The ideal candidate is a self-starter; he will own the account."

## Example

From an advert for a customer support specialist.

Compliant:

```markdown
You have at least a year of experience answering customers by chat or email, you write clearly,
and you stay calm with an upset customer. You'll join a team of six who share a weekly rota and
review tricky tickets together.
```

Violating:

```markdown
You're a recent graduate with 1-3 years of experience, a digital native who'll fit right in with
our young, energetic team.
```

## Before you commit

Reread every advert, post and shared section you created or changed, sentence by sentence,
including taglines, hashtags and the team section. Rewrite any wording about age, career stage,
generation, a cap on years, gender or another protected characteristic as a skill, a minimum or a
description of the work, then check again.
