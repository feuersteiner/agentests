# Balanced investment promotions

## The rule

Promotions for investment products give a balanced picture: benefits such as returns or growth are accompanied in the body of the promotion by the relevant risks, not left to a generic footnote.

The relevant risks always include that the investment's value can fall as well as rise, so the investor could get back less than they put in, and, beside a past return figure, that past performance is not a reliable guide to future returns. They are stated in plain words in the main copy, next to the claims of returns or growth they balance: a footnote, small print, an asterisked note or a bare "Capital at risk" label is not enough. Each piece shown on its own, such as one social ad, a push notification or an in-app card, is a promotion and carries its own risk statement.

Apply it to every promotion you write or edit, for the whole task: headlines, body copy, buttons,
and the front matter (title, description, subject, preheader).

## Why

UK financial promotions must be fair, clear and not misleading, and give a balanced impression of
benefits and risks (FCA, COBS 4). A reader who skims sees the headline and the first lines, not the
footer. A growth story with "Capital at risk" tacked on at the end tells them the upside and hides
the downside, however true each line is.

## What counts

Claims that need a risk statement beside them, in the same section or piece:

- "grow your money", "make your money work harder", "beat inflation", "do better than cash";
- any past return figure ("returned 31% over three years", "up 12% last year"): add the
  past-performance warning as well;
- a projection of what a monthly payment could grow to.

What does not count as the risk statement: "*Capital at risk" under the button, a `<small>` line,
a footnote, a line after the sign-off, or one warning shared by several ads.

A plain statement fits even a short ad: "Its value can fall, so you could get back less than you
put in." is under 70 characters.

## Examples

Compliant:

> **Put £50 a month into the world's markets.** We invest your payment on the 1st of each month.
> Markets rise and fall, so the value of what you hold can go down as well as up, and you could get
> back less than you put in.
>
> **How the fund has done.** It returned 31% over the three years to 30 June. Past performance is
> not a reliable guide to future returns.

Violating:

> **Watch your money grow.** The fund returned 31% in three years, far more than a savings
> account. Start investing today!
>
> <small>*Capital at risk.</small>

## Before you commit

Read each promotion you changed from the top. For every claim of returns or growth, find the
sentence beside it that says the value can fall and the reader could get back less. For every past
figure, find the past-performance warning. In a file of several ads or a push notification and a
card, check each piece on its own. Fix what is missing, then check again.
