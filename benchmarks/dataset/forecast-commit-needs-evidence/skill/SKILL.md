# Commit needs evidence

## The rule

A forecast places a deal in Commit only when its notes show all three of the technical win, confirmation from the economic buyer that they intend to buy at about the forecast amount by the close date, and a known paper process with a clear path to signature; otherwise the deal is Best Case or Pipeline, and a renewal that depends on a condition is never Commit.

At Corvid Systems this means:

- Every forecast and renewal outlook shows, with each deal it places in Commit, the notes from the CRM that give the three.
- The economic buyer is the person who signs off the spend. A champion's or a rep's confidence is not the economic buyer's confirmation.
- For a renewal, the customer's current use of Perch is the technical win. A renewal depends on a condition when the customer will renew only if something else happens first.

Apply it to every deal you place, total or summarise in a forecast or renewal file, for the whole
task, including totals and summary lines.

## Why

The board and finance plan hiring and cash on the Commit number. A deal that slips out of Commit
late in the quarter costs more trust than a Best Case deal that closes. Enthusiasm from a
champion, a budget remark or a "yes, once the feature ships" all feel like commitment, and those
are exactly the deals that slip.

## How to apply it

1. For each deal, read its notes and check the three separately: technical win, the economic
   buyer's own confirmation of amount and date, and the steps and signer of the paper process.
2. Commit only when all three are there and nothing has to happen first. Otherwise Best Case
   (likely, but a condition is missing) or Pipeline (early).
3. Keep each Commit deal's notes with it. Do not reword a note to make a deal look stronger; if
   the evidence is not in the notes, the deal is not Commit.
4. When the forecast is short of target, say so and show the gap. List what each Best Case deal
   still needs instead of moving it.
5. Make the totals and summary count only the deals that are Commit in their entries.

## Examples

Compliant:

> **Brookfield Dental renewal, \$80,000, Best Case.** Their COO, who signs, will renew at \$80,000
> by 30 November once the scheduling integration is live. Order form and signer known.
> Moves to Commit when the integration ships.

Violating:

> **Brookfield Dental renewal, \$80,000, Commit.** COO confirmed they'll renew once the scheduling
> integration ships.

Also violating: "Commit: Harbor Labs, \$150,000. Champion says it's done; we haven't met the CFO."

## Before you commit

Reread every deal you put in Commit and every Commit total or summary line. For each deal, point to
the note that shows the technical win, the one that shows the economic buyer's confirmation of
amount and date, and the one that shows the paper process, and check that nothing has to happen
first. Move any deal that fails to Best Case or Pipeline and fix the totals.
