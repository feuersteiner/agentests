# Say exactly what each piece of data is used for

## The rule

A privacy notice describes each purpose concretely enough that a reader knows which data is used for what, and avoids vague purposes such as "to develop new services", "for research purposes" or "to personalise your experience" and hedges like "may", "might", "some" and "possible" when describing what is done.

This applies to every privacy notice and to every privacy section or statement, such as a cookies and analytics section or a short privacy note for an app or a signup page. A concrete purpose names the data and what is done with it, for example "we record which screens you open in the app to find the onboarding steps where people get stuck". A purpose such as "to improve our services", "to provide the service" or "for analytics" on its own is vague, and so is one list of uses given for all the data at once. The hedge words are fine when they describe what the reader can do ("you may opt out at any time") or are used in another sense; they are not fine when they describe what the company collects, uses, shares or keeps.

Hold to it for the whole task, in every privacy text you touch, including when you only shorten
or reorganise one.

## Why

The GDPR requires privacy information to be clear, and the EU data protection authorities'
transparency guidelines name these exact phrases as not clear enough: a reader cannot tell what
"new services" are, or whether "may" means we do it or not. Vague purposes also read as if we are
keeping our options open, which costs trust. We know precisely what we do with each piece of data,
so the notice can say it.

## How to write a purpose

- Name the data, then the use: "Your email address: to sign you in and send password resets."
- One purpose per data use. Do not merge several uses into one generic line to save space;
  shorten with a table or a list of data and uses instead.
- Say what you do, in the present tense: "we use", "we send", "we keep". Not "we may use".
- Saying what data is not used for ("not used for advertising") is welcome.
- "You may", "you can" and "some" about the reader's choices or about features are fine.

Watch for these stock phrases, which agents write by default: "to provide, maintain and improve
our services", "to personalise your experience", "for research and development", "for analytics
purposes", "we may share your information with trusted partners", "we may collect certain
information automatically".

## Examples

Compliant:

> **Delivery tracking.** We use your postcode and the courier's scan events to show where your
> parcel is. The courier receives your name, address and phone number to deliver it.

Violating:

> We may use the information we collect to provide and improve our services, for research
> purposes and to personalise your experience. Some data may be shared with trusted partners.

## Before you commit

Read each privacy text you changed, from top to bottom. For every statement of why data is
collected, used or shared, check that it names the data and the concrete use, and that no "may",
"might", "could", "some", "certain" or "possible" softens what we do. Rewrite any that fail from
the facts you have, then check again.
