# Privacy notice

Last updated: 3 March 2026

This notice explains what personal data Tessellate collects when you use the Tessellate web app at
app.tessellate.example and this website, what we use each piece of data for, who else receives
it, how long we keep it and what rights you have. We have tried to explain everything in this
notice in detail, so it is quite long. If anything in it is unclear, please write to us and we
will explain it.

## Who we are

Tessellate B.V. ("Tessellate", "we", "us") makes Tessellate, an online whiteboard for product
teams. We are registered in the Netherlands, at Oudegracht 112, 3511 AV Utrecht. We decide what
personal data is collected through the web app and this website and what it is used for, which
makes us the controller of that data under the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR).

Our privacy lead is Ilse Verbeek. You can reach her at privacy@tessellate.example about anything
in this notice.

## Your account

When you sign up, you give us your name, your email address and a password. We store a hash of the
password, never the password itself.

We use your name and email address to create your account and to sign you in. We use your email
address to send you password reset emails when you ask for one, and security emails, for example
when your account signs in from a new country. We do not use your account email address to send
you marketing emails. If you want our newsletter, you sign up for it separately (see below).

## Your workspace and boards

Your workspace has a name, a list of members and the role of each member (owner, editor or
viewer). We use this to show members to each other and to decide who can open and edit each board.

The content of your boards, meaning sticky notes, documents, images and comments, is stored so
that we can show each board to the people it is shared with. We also use it to build the search
index for your workspace, so that members can search their own boards. Board content is not used
for advertising. Tessellate staff do not open your boards unless you ask our support team to look
at a board to help you with a problem.

## Billing

If your workspace is on a paid plan, the workspace owner gives us a billing contact, a company name
and, for companies in the EU, a VAT number. We use these to issue invoices and to charge the
subscription.

Card payments are handled by Stripe. Stripe stores your card details and charges the card. We
never see the full card number; we only see the card brand and its last four digits, which we show
on your invoices so that you can tell which card was charged.

## Support

When you email support@tessellate.example, we use your message and your email address to answer
you. Once a month our support lead tags the month's tickets by topic to count the five most common
problems, which the product team then works on. Support messages are not used to train any model.

## Keeping your account safe

When you sign in, we record your IP address and the type of browser and device you use. We use
this to email you when your account signs in from a country it has not signed in from before, and
to lock your account for 15 minutes after 10 failed sign-in attempts in a row. We do not use this
data for advertising or to track where you are.

## Newsletter

If you sign up for our newsletter on this website, we use the email address you give us to send
you the monthly product newsletter, and for nothing else. Every newsletter has an unsubscribe link
at the bottom; when you use it, we remove your address from the list.

## Cookies and analytics

We set three cookies:

- `tsl_session` keeps you signed in. It expires after 14 days.
- `tsl_csrf` stops other websites from submitting forms in your name. It is deleted when you close
  your browser.
- `tsl_analytics_id` is set only if you click "Accept analytics" on the cookie banner. It is a
  random ID that links the pages and features one browser uses, so that we can count how many
  people use each feature and see at which step people leave the sign-up form. It expires after
  13 months.

If you accept analytics, the web app also records which feature you used, when, and your
workspace's plan. Our product team reviews these counts every quarter to decide which features to
simplify or remove. We do not build profiles of individual users from them and never share them
outside Tessellate. We set no advertising or social media cookies.

You can change your choice at any time with the "Cookie settings" link at the bottom of every page.

## Who receives your data

We use a small number of other companies to run Tessellate. Each one receives only the data it
needs for the job we give it:

- Amazon Web Services hosts the web app and stores all account and board data in its Frankfurt
  data centre.
- Stripe receives your billing details and card details to charge your subscription.
- Postmark receives your name and email address to deliver our emails, including the newsletter.

We do not sell personal data, and we do not share it with advertisers.

## How long we keep your data

- Account data: for as long as your account exists, plus 30 days.
- Workspace data and board content: until you delete it, plus 30 days in our backups.
- Invoices and billing details: 7 years, because Dutch tax law requires it.
- Support messages: 2 years after the ticket is closed.
- Sign-in records (IP address, browser and device): 90 days.
- Newsletter address: until you unsubscribe.

## Your rights

Under the GDPR you have the right to ask for a copy of your personal data, to have it corrected or
deleted, to restrict how we use it, to object to how we use it and to receive it in a portable
format. To use any of these rights, email privacy@tessellate.example. We answer within one month.

You also have the right to complain to the Dutch data protection authority, the Autoriteit
Persoonsgegevens, at autoriteitpersoonsgegevens.nl.

## Changes to this notice

When we change this notice, we update the date at the top. If a change affects how we use data you
have already given us, we email every workspace owner at least 30 days before the change takes
effect.
