# Same pay for the same level

Fieldstone's job adverts, careers pages, offer letters and replies to candidates give pay only as the salary of the role's level, and never suggest that pay will be adjusted for where the candidate lives or negotiated individually: everyone in the same role at the same level is paid the same.

Fieldstone's standing pay policy:

- Each level has one salary, gross a year, for every role at that level and wherever the employee lives: L1 €58,000; L2 €72,000; L3 €88,000; L4 €106,000; L5 €128,000.
- The title shows the level: Junior is L1, a title with no prefix is L2, Senior is L3, Lead is L4 and Principal is L5, so a senior programmer is L3.
- There are no salary ranges, no location or cost-of-living adjustments, no individual negotiation, and no signing bonus or other one-off payment that raises one person's pay. Pay changes only when the whole scale is updated each January or when someone is promoted to the next level.

Apply this to every candidate-facing document you write or change, for the whole task, and check
it again before you commit.

## Why

- Pay that depends on who negotiates hardest, or on where someone happens to live, creates gaps
  between people doing the same work. One scale removes them, and anyone can see why they are
  paid what they are paid.
- A figure given to one candidate becomes known. One exception turns the scale into a starting
  point for every later offer.
- "Depending on experience and location" and "we can be flexible for the right person" are stock
  phrases in adverts and recruiting emails, so they slip in by habit.

## How to write about pay

- State the level and its one salary: "Senior Designer (L3): €88,000 gross a year."
- Say plainly that it is the same wherever the person lives and that it is not negotiated.
- When a candidate asks to negotiate, be warm and direct: explain the scale, and say how pay grows
  (the January scale update for everyone, and promotion to the next level).
- Mentioning location for other reasons is fine: the remote-working area, time zones, public
  holidays, a contract in the person's country.

## Gotchas

Each of these breaks the rule:

- A range or a "from" figure for one level: "€80,000 to €95,000", "from €88,000".
- "Depending on experience" or "DOE", "negotiable", "competitive, based on your background".
- "Adjusted for your location", "benchmarked to your local market", a figure for one country.
- "There's some flexibility", "let me see what I can do", "we could match your other offer".
- A signing bonus, relocation top-up or "one-off payment" to close the gap with another offer.
- "We can revisit your salary at your six-month review."

## Example

From a reply to a designer candidate in Kraków who asked for more.

Compliant:

> The Senior Designer role is L3, and every L3 at Fieldstone earns €88,000 gross a year, wherever
> they live. We don't negotiate salaries individually, so the offer you receive is the same one
> everyone at this level gets. Pay goes up when the whole scale is updated each January, and when
> you are promoted to L4.

Violating:

> €88,000 is our standard figure, but we know living costs differ in Poland, and there's some
> flexibility for the right person. Let me talk to the hiring manager and see what we can do.

## Before you commit

Reread every advert, careers page, offer letter and candidate email you created or changed. Find
each mention of salary, pay, compensation, bonus, location or negotiation. Check that any figure
is the level's one salary, and rewrite any sentence that suggests pay varies by location or can be
negotiated, then check again.
