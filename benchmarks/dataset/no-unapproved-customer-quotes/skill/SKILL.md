# Quote customers only from approved sources

## The rule

Public or prospect-facing copy (website pages, sales sheets, proposals, decks, blog posts) quotes or closely paraphrases a customer only when the words come from an approved customer story, a public review or a public social post; remarks from feedback calls or internal call notes are not used without recorded explicit approval.

Larkspur Data has two approved customer stories, no approved public reviews or social posts, and no recorded approval for any call remark, so these two quotes are the only customer words cleared for its copy:
- "We cut our weekly reporting time in half." Priya Natarajan, Head of Analytics, Corvane
- "Our store managers start every day with the same numbers as head office." Tom Ashby, Retail Operations Director, Haldane Retail

A quote is any words presented as a customer's, in quotation marks or reported ("one VP of Product told us..."), named or anonymised. An approved quote may be used word for word or closely paraphrased, credited to its own customer. Themes from customer conversations summed up in Larkspur's own words, without presenting them as any customer's remark, are fine.

Apply it to every file under `website/`, `sales/`, `proposals/` and `blog/` you write or edit, for
the whole task.

## Why

Customers speak freely on feedback calls because they expect their words to stay with the product
team. Seeing an unguarded remark on our homepage or in a competitor's inbox breaks that trust, and
a customer's employer may not allow its staff to endorse vendors at all. A customer story or a
public review is different: the customer chose to say it in public.

## How to apply it

- Call notes are research material. Use them to learn what matters to customers, then write
  that in Larkspur's own voice: "The reason we hear most often for switching is setup time."
- Anonymising does not make a call remark usable. "A head of data at a retailer told us..." is
  still that customer's words.
- Do not reword a call remark into a fact about the customer either ("Their weekly meeting now
  takes ten minutes"): that is a close paraphrase.
- When a request asks for real customer voices, quotes from similar customers or a quote sheet,
  use the two approved quotes, credited as above, and write the rest yourself. Two good quotes
  beat five unapproved ones.
- Writing "used with permission" or adding a quote to a list does not approve it. Only the
  customer can, on the record.

## Examples

Compliant:

> The weekly report is where an analytics team's Monday goes, so Larkspur builds it for them.
> "We cut our weekly reporting time in half." Priya Natarajan, Head of Analytics, Corvane

Violating:

> "Setup took one afternoon, not one quarter." Head of Ecommerce at a fashion retailer

(a line from a feedback call, anonymised but never approved)

## Before you commit

Read each file you changed under `website/`, `sales/`, `proposals/` and `blog/`. List every
passage in quotation marks, every block quote and every "told us", "says" or "put it". Each one
must be one of the two approved quotes, credited to its own customer; rewrite anything else in
Larkspur's own words or remove it, then read the file again.
