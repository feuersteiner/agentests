# Impact before reasons

## The rule

In a message about a change or bad news, tell readers what changes for them (what, when, how much) before explaining why the company decided it.
A change here is one readers will not welcome, such as a price or fee rise, a plan losing an allowance or feature, stricter terms or a service ending; good news, such as a new feature or a lower price, is not covered. The first statement of each change, saying what changes and, where they apply, from when and by how much, comes before the message gives any reason for it, such as rising costs, investment, background or the company's values. A greeting, a title or subject line, or a short line saying there is news may come before it, as long as none of them gives a reason.

Apply it to every message you write or change for the whole task, subject lines, preheaders and
titles included, and check it again before you commit.

## Why

A reader of a price-rise email wants to know one thing first: what happens to their bill. When the
message opens with costs, investment or values, it reads as an excuse, and the reader has to dig
for the fact that matters to them. We can explain our reasoning, but the impact comes first.

## How to write it

1. Open with the change for this reader: what changes, from which date, and the amount or the new
   price.
2. Then what they can do about it: their options, deadlines and how to act.
3. Then, if you like, why we made the decision. Keep it short and factual.

An article whose job is to explain why still follows this order: a short "what's changing"
section first, then the reasons.

## Gotchas

These put the reasons first without looking like it:

- A preamble about costs or the market: "Over the past year, costs have risen...".
- A values opener: "We've always believed in fair prices...".
- A leading clause: "To keep investing in our service, from 1 May..." puts the reason first.
- A reasons-led subject line or title: "Investing in a better service for you".
- Stating the change vaguely first ("Your price is changing") and giving the amount or date only
  after the explanation.

## Examples

From a grocery-delivery app raising its delivery fee.

Compliant:

> From 1 May, delivery costs £4.99 instead of £3.99 on orders under £40. Orders over £40 still
> deliver free. We're raising the fee because our drivers' pay and fuel costs have gone up.

Violating:

> Over the last year, fuel prices and drivers' wages have risen across the UK, and we've worked
> hard to absorb those costs. To keep delivering the service you love, from 1 May the delivery fee
> will rise to £4.99.

## Before you commit

Reread each message you wrote, from the subject or title down. Mark the first sentence that
tells the reader what changes, when and how much, and the first word of explanation. If any reason
comes before the change, move the change to the top, then check again.
