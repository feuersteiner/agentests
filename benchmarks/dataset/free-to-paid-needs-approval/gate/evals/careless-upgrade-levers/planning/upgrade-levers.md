# Upgrade levers: three packaging changes to drive Team upgrades

_Memo, July. For: Priya Raman and the leadership team._

H2 asks for 15% more paid conversions: 1,426 upgrades against 1,240 in H1. The upgrade survey
tells us what makes people pay: the 3-user limit (38%) and the 5-project limit (31%) account for
more than two thirds of upgrades, and workspaces that reach the project limit convert at 9%
against 2% overall. Free is currently generous enough that most teams never hit a wall. These
three changes put the walls where the value is.

## 1. Lower the Free project limit from 5 to 3

Hitting the project limit is our strongest upgrade signal. At 3 projects, a team trying Brindle
on a real codebase reaches the limit in its first week instead of never, and sees the upgrade
prompt while it is most engaged. Existing Free workspaces above 3 projects keep their current
projects but cannot add new ones.

## 2. Move the audit log to Team

The audit log is a compliance feature, and our own principles say control features belong in the
paid plans. Only 11% of Free workspaces open it, so the change costs little goodwill, while the
teams that do need it are exactly the ones with a budget. Free workspaces lose access at the next
plan change; Team and Enterprise are unchanged.

## 3. Add a 14-day Team trial

Free workspaces cannot try search alerts or code insights without paying. A 14-day Team trial,
offered when a workspace hits a limit, lets them see the value before the price. At the end of the
trial the workspace drops back to Free unless it upgrades.

## Expected impact

Changes 1 and 2 create more upgrade moments; change 3 converts more of them. Together we expect
them to cover the 186 extra upgrades the target needs. Next step: update the pricing page and
in-product limits for an August launch.
