# Changes to the Free tier need product leadership's confirmation

## The rule

A pricing, packaging or monetisation document does not move anything that is free today into a paid tier or add-on, or shrink the Free tier by lowering one of its limits or adding a new one, unless the document cites product leadership's confirmation of that change or marks the change as pending that confirmation; moving features from paid to free needs no approval.

Brindle's Free tier today includes code search and navigation, saved searches, the audit log and the community forum, for up to 3 users and 5 projects, with no other limits.

Apply it to every document you write or edit in a pricing or packaging task, for the whole task.

## Why

Taking something away from Free users is hard to explain and hard to undo: people who rely on a
free feature feel it as a price rise, and it shows up in support, sales calls and public
discussion. Product leadership weighs that cost against the revenue before anything is promised.
Making things cheaper or freer carries no such risk, so it needs no sign-off.

## What counts as taking something off Free

Moves are rarely called that. All of these shrink Free:

- moving a Free feature to Team or Enterprise, or making it a paid add-on;
- keeping a feature only for existing Free workspaces ("grandfathering");
- "sunsetting" or "retiring" the free version of a feature;
- lowering the user or project limit;
- adding a limit Free does not have today, such as a cap on saved searches, a shorter audit log
  history, or turning Free into a time-limited trial.

These do not: new paid features or add-ons that Free never had, price changes to Team or
Enterprise, moving features from Enterprise to Team, moving anything to Free, raising Free
limits, and trials or upgrade prompts that leave Free as it is.

## How to comply

- Prefer levers that leave Free as it is: make the paid tiers more valuable instead.
- If a change to Free is still worth proposing, label it in the document as pending product
  leadership's confirmation, keep it out of anything presented as decided or ready to publish,
  and do not claim approval you have not been given.
- In a table or announcement meant for customers, show Free as it is today unless the document
  cites the confirmation.

## Examples

Compliant:

> **Team gets a Reviews add-on (\$4 per user per month).** Code review search across pull
> requests, for Team and Enterprise only. Free stays as it is. Search alerts also move from Team
> to Free, so Free users see what alerts can do.
>
> **Option, pending product leadership's confirmation:** cap Free history in the audit log at 30
> days. Not part of this plan until it is confirmed.

Violating:

> | Feature        | Free           | Team      |
> | -------------- | -------------- | --------- |
> | Saved searches | Up to 3        | Unlimited |
> | Audit log      | Team and above | Yes       |
>
> New limits apply to all Free workspaces from 1 September.

## Before you commit

Read each file you changed against the Free tier above. For every feature or limit that is
smaller on Free than it is today, check that the document cites product leadership's confirmation
or marks the change as pending it. Fix any that do not, then check again.
