# Describe the offsetting, never a neutral or reduced impact

## The rule

Do not claim a product, delivery or service is carbon neutral, climate neutral, net zero or has a reduced climate impact on the basis of offsetting; describe the offsetting itself, factually, instead.

Driftwood Coffee Co. funds a certified forestry project to offset the estimated emissions of its deliveries, and it holds no lifecycle data and no measured reduction of any of its emissions, so any such claim about Driftwood would rest on offsets alone. Driftwood's copy therefore never calls its coffee, subscriptions, deliveries or the company carbon neutral, climate neutral, net zero, climate positive or low-carbon, and never says their emissions or footprint are cancelled out, erased or reduced. Describing the offsetting factually means saying that Driftwood offsets the estimated emissions of its deliveries by funding the project, never that the offsets cover the coffee itself, its growing, roasting or packaging, or all of Driftwood's emissions.

Apply it to every page, email and interface string you write or edit, in every language, for the
whole task: titles, descriptions, headings, subject lines, preheaders, buttons and body copy.

## Why

Buying credits does not change the emissions of a parcel or a bag of coffee. EU consumer law
(Directive (EU) 2024/825) lists a neutral, reduced or positive climate claim based on offsetting
among the practices that are always unfair. Saying exactly what we offset, and how, is allowed,
and it is the claim we can stand behind.

## What to write instead

1. Say what is offset: the estimated emissions of each delivery, from the roastery to the door.
2. Say how: we fund the Selva Nueva reforestation project in Peru, which is certified and audited.
3. Use the figures in `docs/climate.md` if you need numbers, and no others.
4. If it helps, say what we have not measured. Never turn offsetting into an outcome.

## Gotchas

- Short lines turn into slogans: "Carbon-neutral delivery", "Climate-positive coffee",
  "Net zero shipping". Each is the claim the rule forbids, however true the offsetting is.
- Implied neutrality: "cancels out your coffee's footprint", "zero-emission parcels",
  "footprint-free", "guilt-free shipping". Translations too: "klimaneutral", "CO2-neutral".
- Implied reduction: "lower-carbon delivery", "we've cut the climate impact of every order".
- Stretching the scope: "we offset your coffee from farm to cup" or "we offset all our emissions".
  Only deliveries are offset.

## Examples

Compliant:

> From 1 December we offset the estimated emissions of every delivery by funding Selva Nueva, a
> certified reforestation project in Peru. We have not yet measured the footprint of the coffee
> itself.

Violating:

> Every Driftwood delivery is now carbon neutral: our forest partner cancels out the footprint of
> your coffee, from farm to cup.

## Before you commit

Read each page, email and locale file you changed and list every mention of climate, carbon, CO2,
emissions, footprint, neutral, net zero, positive, offset, planet and their translations. For each,
check that it describes the offsetting of delivery emissions as offsetting, or says nothing about
the impact. Rewrite what is left, then read the file once more.
