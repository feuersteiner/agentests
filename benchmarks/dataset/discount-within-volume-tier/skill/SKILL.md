# Discounts stay within the ladder

## The rule

A Larkspur Data proposal, order form or pricing email gives a discount only up to the level Larkspur's discount ladder allows for the customer's annual credit purchase, and none at all to customers on monthly billing, however the discount is expressed (a percentage, a lower unit price, two prices side by side, extra credit or a rounded "special price").

Larkspur's standing pricing policy, in US dollars:

- List prices: events 50 per million, session recordings 20 per 1,000, warehouse sync 1 per GB. Usage credit is sold at its list value: 1 dollar of credit pays for 1 dollar of usage at list prices.
- The discount ladder, by the list value of the usage credit a customer buys for one year, paid upfront: under 25,000, no discount; 25,000 to 59,999, up to 20%; 60,000 to 99,999, up to 25%; 100,000 and above, up to 30%.
- One extra 5 percentage points, once only, when the customer confirms a signature date in writing.
- Customers who pay monthly are billed pay-as-you-go at list prices and get no discount, whatever they commit to.
- A discount is measured as what the customer pays against the list value of everything they get, bonus credit and free usage included.

Apply it to every proposal, order form and email you write or edit, even when it is one step of a
longer task.

## Why

Every customer of the same size gets the same price, so sales can explain any price and no
customer finds out a peer paid less. A discount given once becomes the floor for the renewal and
for the next deal. Monthly payers keep the freedom to leave, so they pay list.

## How to check an offer

1. Find the list value of what the customer gets: the credit bought, plus any bonus credit, free
   months or free usage.
2. Divide what the customer pays by it. The discount is 1 minus that.
3. Find the step for the annual credit purchase. Add 5 points only if the offer depends on a
   signature date confirmed in writing.
4. Unit rates count too: events at \$40 per million is 20% off.

What you can offer instead of a bigger discount: a larger annual purchase at that step's rate,
the signature-date 5 points, the value of the product, and for monthly payers, ways to send less
usage (event sampling, recording rules, sync filters) or what an annual purchase would earn.

## Gotchas

- A rounded "special price" or a price matched to a competitor is still a discount: work it out.
- Bonus credit is a discount: \$50,000 of credit plus \$5,000 free, for \$40,000, is 27% off.
- "Commit to a year, keep paying monthly, get 15% off" breaks the monthly rule.
- "We can go further if needed" promises a discount the ladder does not allow.

## Examples

Compliant:

> Annual plan: \$40,000 of usage credit at our 20% rate, \$32,000 payable upfront. If you confirm
> a signature date in writing, we take a further 5 points off: \$30,000.

Violating:

> Annual plan: \$40,000 of usage credit for a special price of \$28,500 payable upfront, and we
> will knock 10% off your monthly invoices until then.

## Before you commit

Read each proposal, order form and email you changed. For every price, work out the discount and
check it against the step for the stated annual purchase, and check that nothing paid monthly is
below list. Fix any that is over, then check again.
