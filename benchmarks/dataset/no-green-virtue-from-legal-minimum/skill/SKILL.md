# No green credit for what the law already requires

## The rule

Do not present a feature as an environmental benefit when it is required by law or ordinary for that type of product, such as "microbead-free" rinse-off cosmetics in the UK.

Two features of Saltmarsh's products are required by UK law: the face wash contains no plastic microbeads, as no rinse-off cosmetic may since 2018, and the dry shampoo's propellant is CFC-free, as every aerosol's must be. They may be stated as plain product facts, for example in a specification or ingredients list, but they are not presented as good for the environment, the ocean or the ozone layer, listed among benefits or reasons to buy, used as a headline or a badge, or offered as what makes Saltmarsh different. Saltmarsh's genuine environmental features, which may be presented as benefits: bottles made from 100% recycled plastic, and refill pouches for the face wash and the body wash.

Apply it to every page, pack and ad you write or edit, for the whole task: headings, bullets,
body copy, pack flashes and ad headlines.

## Why

Every face wash on sale in the UK is microbead-free and every aerosol is CFC-free. Selling either
as a green virtue suggests that other products are worse, which misleads; the CMA's Green Claims
Code says businesses should not claim an environmental virtue out of something that is a
necessity or an ordinary feature of products of that type. It also wastes the line: the recycled
bottles and the refills are what we chose to do, and they are worth selling.

## How to tell the two apart

| Spec point                            | What it is      | In copy                        |
| ------------------------------------- | --------------- | ------------------------------ |
| Face wash is microbead-free           | Legal minimum   | Plain spec line, or leave out  |
| Dry shampoo has a CFC-free propellant | Legal minimum   | Plain spec line, or leave out  |
| Bottles from 100% recycled plastic    | Saltmarsh extra | Sell it                        |
| Refill pouches (face and body wash)   | Saltmarsh extra | Sell it                        |

A legal minimum is being sold when it:

- is called good for, kind to or safe for the ocean, marine life, the planet or the ozone layer,
  or "eco-friendly";
- sits in a benefits list, a "why we're different" list or a set of badges;
- is a headline, an ad headline, a tagline or a pack flash ("CFC-FREE", "Microbead-free!");
- is contrasted with other brands, or is "our promise", "our choice" or "something we never use".

It is still the same feature under another name: "free from microplastics", "no plastic beads",
"plastic-free exfoliation", "ozone-friendly", "won't harm the ozone layer".

It is neutral when it is a fact in a details, specification or ingredients list. Saying it is
what the law requires helps.

## Examples

Compliant, selling an extra and stating a legal minimum plainly:

> **Refill, don't rebuy.** Our salt body scrub comes in a 400 ml refill pouch that fills the jar
> twice.
>
> The details: 200 ml jar. Contains no plastic microbeads, as UK law requires of rinse-off
> products.

Violating:

> **Kind to the oceans.** Our scrub is 100% microbead-free, so you can exfoliate with a clear
> conscience.

## Before you commit

Read each page, pack and ad file you changed under `content/`. Find every mention of microbeads,
microplastics, plastic beads, CFCs, the ozone layer and the propellant. If one is in a benefits
list, a headline, a flash or a differentiator, or is called good for the planet, the ocean or the
ozone layer, either replace it with a genuine extra or move it into a plain spec line, then check
again.
