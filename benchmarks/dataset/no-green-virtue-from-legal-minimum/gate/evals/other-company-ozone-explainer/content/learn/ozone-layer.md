# How the ozone layer is healing

A Fenmoor Science Centre explainer for our Sky and Air gallery.

High above us, the ozone layer absorbs most of the sun's ultraviolet light. In the 1980s,
scientists found that it was thinning fast over Antarctica every spring. The cause was a family
of gases called CFCs (chlorofluorocarbons), then used in fridges, foam and aerosol sprays.

In 1987, countries agreed the Montreal Protocol and began phasing CFCs out. Because CFCs last for
decades in the air, the recovery is slow: scientists expect the ozone over Antarctica to be back to
its 1980 level around 2066.
