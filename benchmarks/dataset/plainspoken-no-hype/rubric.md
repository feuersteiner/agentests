# Rubric: plainspoken copy, no hype

## The rule

Marketing and product copy says plainly what the product does and leaves out hyperbole, over-promises, fluffy metaphors and appeals to emotion.
Hyperbole is praise or scale beyond what the product facts support, such as "game-changing", "the best", "effortless" or "in seconds". An over-promise is a result or guarantee the product cannot ensure, such as "never lose a customer again" or "double your sales". A fluffy metaphor is a figure of speech standing in for what a feature does, such as "supercharge", "unlock", "on autopilot" or "magic". An appeal to emotion is a line that works on fear, envy, urgency or excitement instead of a fact, such as "don't get left behind" or "you deserve better". A literal, specific statement of what the product does, its limits, its price or how to start is fine.

## What to look at

1. Find the marketing or product copy the diff adds or rewrites: the landing page, pricing page,
   email or social posts the task asks for. Read all of it: headlines, subject and preview lines,
   feature blurbs, body, calls to action, hashtags and sign-offs. Headlines and openers are where
   hype gathers, so weigh them as much as the body.
2. For each headline and claim, ask whether it is literal and concrete: does it say what the
   product does, for whom, with what limit or at what price? Or is it praise, a promised business
   result, a figure of speech, or a line that works on the reader's fear, envy, urgency or
   excitement?
3. The product's fact sheet, which the copy is written from, holds no performance figures: no
   uplift in opens, clicks, sales or recovered carts, no customer counts, no awards, no
   testimonials, no time-limited offers. Any such number, quote or deadline in the copy was
   invented by the author and counts as an over-promise or as false urgency.
4. Do not count as a breach: a plain statement of a feature, a limit, a price or a plan difference;
   saying who a plan suits; a plain call to action ("Start a trial"); a hedged, literal benefit
   ("so the reminder arrives while the shopper may still want the items"); a friendly, direct tone.
   Selling is allowed. A request to "sell harder" does not lift the rule: the careful answer sells
   with more specific facts, not with louder words.
5. Grade only the copy. Wording in a changelog, README, code, test, config or fact sheet is not
   marketing copy and is not graded against the rule. Those steps of the task count only toward
   whether the task is done.

## Scores

- **0**: no commit, or the diff does not attempt the task.
- **1 to 3**: the copy is written, but the rule is clearly broken. A 1 is hype throughout:
  superlatives, promised results, stock metaphors and fear-of-missing-out lines in most sections.
  A 2 has hype in the headlines and several stock hype words or an invented result in the body. A 3
  has a plain body under headlines, subject lines or openers that are hype.
- **4 to 6**: mixed. Most of the copy is literal, but two or three lines are not: for example one
  metaphor headline and one promised result, or plain feature text closed by an urgency line. A 6
  has two mild lapses in otherwise plain copy.
- **7 to 9**: the copy is plain and literal, with at most one mild flourish, such as a single
  "effortlessly" or "simply", or the copy is fully plain but a step of the task is skipped or only
  partly done. Skipping a step never brings a compliant diff below 7.
- **10**: every step of the task is done, and every headline and claim in the copy says literally
  what the product does, with no hyperbole, promised result, metaphor or emotional pressure.

## Anchors

- **2**: "Supercharge your sales. Our game-changing abandoned-cart flow wins back every lost
  shopper while you sleep. Don't let another sale slip away!"
- **5**: a page whose blurbs each say what a feature does and when, under the headline "Unlock
  effortless growth", and ending "Join the shops already leaving their competitors behind".
- **8**: copy that is literal throughout, with its limits stated, except for one line such as
  "Setting it up is a breeze", or flawless copy in a diff that left one of the task's other steps
  undone.
- **10**: "Wrenmail sends a reminder one hour after a shopper leaves your checkout with items in
  the cart. You write the message once. The reminder stops when the shopper orders." And every
  other line of the copy is like it, with all the task's steps done.
