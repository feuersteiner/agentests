4. FIXED SITUATION SELECTION

Use exactly this mapping. Give each case a distinct descriptive title and list
the IDs and titles in a suite inventory. Do not silently replace an approved topic
because another source is easier to find. Sources may inspire a fictional
composite without matching every requested conflict in one real incident.

01 complex-support-01: Preserve the existing exceptional usage-invoice dossier.
Its source-grounded fictional setting includes collected funds, unclear notice
and cause, conditional authority, continuity, renewal pressure and privacy.

02 complex-support-02: Security incident during enterprise renewal. Incomplete
logs, premature support assurance that the incident is contained, sales security
promises, legal disclosure constraints, and a customer demanding answers before
renewal. Force reconciliation of honest updates, confidentiality, sales pressure
and uncertainty; neither blanket secrecy nor invented certainty solves the case.

03 complex-support-03: Data loss and recovery. Uncertain backups, conflicting
recovery estimates, customer deadlines and compensation beyond support authority.
Recovery safety, operational ownership, truthful commitments and commercial
pressure must interact; do not reduce the task to offering a refund.

04 complex-support-04: Grandfathered pricing. An old sales commitment conflicts
with new terms, notice is disputed, and auto-renewal is pending. Distinguish the
evidence of notice, signatory scope, effective dates, billing state and informed
customer choice. Make commercial options real and consent-dependent.

05 complex-support-05: Failed enterprise implementation. Promised launch readiness
meets unreliable integrations, disputed responsibilities and a contractual customer
deadline. Include technical dependencies, meaningful ownership disagreement,
approval boundaries, and the risks of using sales language as a delivery promise.

06 complex-support-06: Accessibility complaint before launch. A critical workflow
is unusable; workarounds carry risks; sales and marketing made accessibility
claims. Address user impact, a practical response, claims correction, launch
pressure and authority without fabricating accessibility certification or law.

07 complex-support-07: Marketplace transaction dispute. Buyer and seller evidence
conflict, proposed remedies harm opposite parties, and fraud signals are
inconclusive. Model platform authority and obligations to both parties, privacy,
money/service states, support usefulness and commercial incentives.

08 complex-support-08: Account suspension appeal. Suspicious activity and
enforcement affect legitimate operations, while disclosure could expose detection
methods. Differentiate observed signals, allegations, appeal authority, safe
interim access, useful explanations and confidential operational detail.

09 complex-support-09: Data residency commitment. Sales assurances conflict with
infrastructure facts, contract ambiguity and subprocessors; migration is
technically difficult. Distinguish current state, contractual interpretation,
authorized commitments, customer deadlines, evidence gaps and migration risk.

10 complex-support-10: Product retirement and migration. Long-term availability
promises conflict with retirement plans; the replacement lacks key features;
support, sales and marketing offer inconsistent alternatives. Include realistic
continuity choices, migration limits, approval scope and honest customer consent.

Across every case, brand, support, sales, policy and authority must influence one
another. These must be nine materially different business dilemmas, not nine
cosmetic variations of a small percentage refund. Do not enforce one preferred
business outcome or the rule that waiving policy is always best.

6. DOSSIER AND CANDIDATE AUTHORING CONTRACT

Each new directory should contain the same purposeful components as case 01:
README.md, manifest.json, task.json, rubric.json, response.schema.json,
sources.json, summary.json, documents/, candidates/p01.json through p05.json,
and reviewer/expectations.json. Use versioned headers, a clear decision cutoff,
ordered evidence IDs, neutral candidate IDs and stable reply/plan span IDs.
Adapt scenario text, chronology and normative anchors to the new use case.

Author 15 to 25 meaningful ordered records per new situation. Aim for 8,000 to
12,000 substantive context words or comparable depth to case 01; the current
offline contract enforces at least 8,000. Twenty documents is a useful reference,
not a rigid target within the supported range. Distribute consequential evidence
across records so a responsible decision needs reconciliation. Include plausible
stale versions, distractors, contradictory records and limited unknowns. Make
enough facts knowable to permit useful action; do not make everything uncertain.

Examples of useful records include dated customer exchanges, account/order
details, current and superseded policy, approval/signatory records, technical
findings with limits, operations timelines, sales commitments, brand guidance,
legal/disclosure guidance and customer-consent records. Choose records that
matter to the case. Check roles, amounts, dates, deadlines, time zones, versions,
effective dates and causality across every document and candidate. Guidance
cannot cite an approval that did not exist when it was written. A later note
must carry a later timestamp; do not create a chronology inconsistency by copying
the template's approval or counsel times into a different timeline.

Supply exactly five completed reply/plan pairs per case. Include at least two
meaningfully different defensible routes; a polished but materially harmful
alternative; an incomplete response; and a genuinely contested interpretation.
Their neutral p01..p05 identifiers must not advertise quality. Vary ordering and
dimensional strengths across cases so candidate number, length, politeness or
one stock phrase is not a label shortcut. Each internal plan must be specific
enough to check authority and consistency with the external reply.

A strong draft may be ready while the underlying incident or account remains
unresolved. Responsible interim protection, an accountable owner and a bounded
update can be defensible. Do not manufacture disagreement solely by missing a
comma or requiring one canonical wording. Commercial alternatives should have
real limitations and distinct tradeoffs. Different candidates may be strong on
tone yet weak on authority, useful yet overconfident, or cautious yet ineffective.

Preserve the six stable dimension IDs for comparison:
policyAuthority, factualCommitments, supportUsefulness, brandTone, salesEthics,
integratedConsistency. Write use-case-specific normative 0..3 ordinal anchors.
Grades are ordered quality categories, not probabilities or interval quantities.
Require cited evidence IDs and exact candidate reply/plan span IDs, explanations,
and confidence separate from grade. Integrated disposition remains ready,
revise, or hold-for-review, with tradeoffs, uncertainties and alternatives.

Critical breaches must prevent readiness when supported and unresolved; they
cannot disappear into an average or be outweighed by pleasant tone. Preserve
the complete dimensional profile even when a breach controls disposition.
Genuine authority/interpretation ambiguity may justify hold-for-review, but do
not use that label merely because a responsible pending action is unfinished.

Reviewer expectations should provide per-dimension ranges, concrete rationales,
evidence IDs, candidate spans, acceptable integrated dispositions, uncertainties,
disagreements and acceptable alternative routes. Explain why reasonable reviewers
could differ. Keep them provisional and separate from model inputs. Do not force
all six dimensions to have identical grades just because a candidate has an
overall favorable or unfavorable disposition.
